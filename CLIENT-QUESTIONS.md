# What We Need From Nero — Private Events Page

Grouped by what it blocks. **§1–§3 block launch.** §4–§6 don't block the page going live but each one measurably moves close rate, and the playbook's whole argument is that these are where deals are won and lost.

A copy-paste email for Matías is at the bottom.

## §1 — Packages & pricing · **BLOCKS LAUNCH**

This is the single biggest gap. The current BentoBox page says nothing about cost, and the playbook's core finding is that vague pricing is the number one reason planners bounce. Right now every price on our page is invented.

1. **Do you have set packages, or is every event quoted custom?** If it's all custom, that's fine — but we need the three or four most common shapes so we can publish something real. What did your last ten events actually look like?
2. For each package: **name, per-guest price, what's included** (courses, service style, bar tier and duration), and **minimum guest count**.
3. Is bar priced **inside** the per-guest number, or as a separate open bar / consumption tab / drink tickets? This is the single most common place a planner gets surprised, so we want to state it plainly.
4. **Room minimums** — is there a food-and-beverage minimum per space, and does it change by day of week or season? (Friday in December vs. Tuesday in February.)
5. Is there a **room rental or setup fee** on top of F&B, or is the minimum the whole story?
6. **Add-ons worth naming:** cake or dessert service, AV for the projector, a DJ, décor, extended hours, coat check. What do people actually buy, and what do they cost?
7. **Deposit:** how much to hold a date, and is it refundable? Cancellation window?
8. **Confirm the 23% service charge and DC's 10% tax** are the complete picture — nothing else appears on a final bill.

> Even rough ranges unblock us. "Most receptions land $65–90 a head all in" is enough to publish a real number, and a real number beats "inquire for pricing" every time.

## §2 — Who owns inquiries · **BLOCKS LAUNCH**

The playbook's strongest single claim: naming one person with a two-hour response SLA moves close rates from the high teens to the low thirties. That's a bigger lever than anything on the page. Our page currently *promises* a two-hour proposal — we need to know it's true before we publish it.

9. **Who owns private event inquiries today?** One named person, or does it rotate? What's their real email — the page currently uses a placeholder `events@nerowashdc.com`.
10. **What's the honest current response time** to a TripleSeat lead? Same day, next day, a few days? No judgment — we need the truth to know whether to publish the two-hour promise or soften it to "same day."
11. **Can they commit to a two-hour SLA during business hours?** If not, say so now and we'll change the copy. Promising it and missing it is worse than not promising it.
12. **Who runs the event on the night?** Is it the same person who sold it? The "one named captain" promise depends on it.

## §3 — TripleSeat access · **BLOCKS LAUNCH**

13. We need the **lead form ID and public key**: TripleSeat → Settings → Lead Forms → **View Setup Codes**. Screenshot of that screen is fine.
14. Can we get a **login or a seat** on the account? Without it we can't verify that test leads arrive, and we can't report on lead-to-booking conversion — which is the number that actually matters.
15. **Can we add three custom questions** to the lead form (`utm_source`, `utm_campaign`, `gclid`)? They can be hidden or short-text and never need to be shown to the guest. This is what lets us tell them "this $8,000 holiday party came from the Meta campaign." Without it, we can count leads but can't connect a booking to an ad.
16. **How many private-event leads came through TripleSeat in the last 90 days, and how many booked?** That's our baseline. Without it we can't prove the new page worked.

## §4 — Operations & policy

17. **Capacity numbers look inverted on the current site** — every space lists *more seated than standing* (50 seated / 30 standing). Standing capacity is normally the higher of the two. Is the site wrong, or is the space genuinely constrained standing? We've carried the numbers through as-is and want to fix it if it's an error.
18. **Sabina** currently says "phone reservation required." Is that still true, or can it be booked through the form like everything else? Every extra step costs bookings.
19. **What's actually bookable when?** Can the Wine Tap Room be bought out during regular service, or only when the restaurant is closed? Are there days or times private events aren't possible?
20. **Outside vendors** — can a client bring their own cake, florist, DJ, photographer? Any fees attached?
21. **The patio** — seasonal only, or year round? Heated?
22. **Parking / transit / accessibility** — is there an elevator or lift between the three levels? Corporate planners ask this early and it kills deals late.

## §5 — Proof & photography

23. **Original event photos.** The page currently hotlinks five images off their website's CDN, which is fragile. We want the original files, ideally: the full room mid-event, a set table, the wine taps, the projector in use, and Sabina. Horizontal, high resolution.
24. **One real testimonial from a past booker** — a corporate planner is ideal — with permission to use their name, title and company. One specific quote outperforms three generic ones.
25. **Any recognizable companies or organizations** who've held events there, with permission to name them. Corporate planners buy on evidence that people like them already did.
26. **A sample menu from a real past event** at each price tier. Planners want to see actual food, not category names.

---

## §6 — Seasonality & the calendar

27. **Which December dates are already booked, and which are still open?** The playbook's strongest ad angle is naming the specific dates that remain. It only works with a live calendar.
28. **What's the realistic capacity ceiling** — how many private events can they physically run in a December week without service suffering? No point driving demand they can't absorb.
29. **What's the biggest event they've done, and what did it bill?** Sets the ceiling for what we tell the market.
30. **Are there dates they'd rather not sell** — holidays they close, a week the owner is away?
